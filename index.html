How it works

No special hardware needed.

All you need is a standard webcam. The system runs entirely on your machine — nothing is sent to a server.

Step 01

Camera sees your hand

MediaPipe detects 21 landmarks on each hand, 30 times per second, at any angle or distance.

Step 02

Gestures are classified

Movement speed, direction, finger count, and pinch distance are all measured against calibrated thresholds before anything fires.

Step 03

macOS gets the command

Confirmed gestures send real keyboard shortcuts and scroll events via AppleScript — the system sees it as normal input.

Step 04

Cooldowns prevent accidents

Every gesture requires multiple consecutive confirmation frames and a cooldown period before it can fire again.

Gesture reference

Everything the system can do.

Eight gesture states, each running in priority order. Higher-priority gestures (like clap) block lower-priority ones from firing at the same time.

Gesture Action How it's detected
Double Clap Toggle system on / off Two hands come within 0.1 units of each other at >0.5 units/frame, twice within 0.8 seconds
Swipe Left Previous browser tab Hand moves >150px horizontally in under 1.5s, confirmed over 6 frames
Swipe Right Next browser tab Same as swipe left, opposite direction
Pinch + Move Scroll page up or down Thumb-to-index distance <0.06, then vertical hand movement drives continuous scroll
Finger Count (1–5) Jump directly to that tab Finger count stable across 20 consecutive frames at 0.9+ confidence
Fist Rest / no action All four fingers curled; system ignores this as the default resting position

Under the hood

Built to not misfire.

Every threshold in the codebase has a comment explaining what problem it was tuned to solve. Stability was the priority, not sensitivity.

Multi-frame confirmation

No gesture fires on a single frame. Each one requires a consistent signal across multiple frames before being accepted.

Gesture priority queue

When multiple signals appear simultaneously, a defined priority order decides which one wins. Clap always beats swipe.

Per-gesture cooldowns

After a gesture fires, that gesture is locked out for 1–1.5 seconds. Swipe once, get one tab switch.

Pinch disables swipe

While the pinch-scroll gesture is active, swipe detection is suspended so scrolling never accidentally switches tabs.

Head calibration

At startup, the system records a neutral head angle over 90 frames. It recalibrates automatically if your posture shifts.

Runs fully local

No cloud, no API keys, no data leaving your machine. MediaPipe runs the hand model on your GPU if available.

Stack

What it's built with.

Python on macOS. The gesture logic is in plain Python — no ML training required beyond the MediaPipe base model.

Interactive demo

Try it in the browser.

Enable your camera, then swipe your hand left or right to switch tabs. Pinch your thumb and index finger together and move your hand up or down to scroll. No data is stored or transmitted.

The new era of space exploration is already underway

Reusable rockets have cut launch costs by over 90 percent in the past decade. Missions once considered a generation away are now scheduled for the late 2020s.

The Artemis program aims to return humans to the lunar surface by 2026 and establish a permanent presence there. A Gateway station in lunar orbit will serve as a staging point for eventual crewed missions to Mars, reducing the risks of deep-space travel by giving crews a known resupply point.

Mars in the near term

Current projections place the first crewed Mars mission between 2028 and 2032. Autonomous habitat construction robots are being tested in analogue environments in Utah and Iceland, designed to prepare landing sites months before any crew arrives.

The economics of asteroid mining are approaching viability. Rare earth elements critical for electronics exist in abundance on certain asteroid types. Several companies have already filed claim frameworks with international bodies.

James Webb updates

The telescope has now identified structured, massive galaxies at redshifts above z=12 — galaxies that should not exist under standard cosmological models. The data suggests either much faster early star formation or a revision of accepted constants. Read the paper.

Urban gardening in small spaces: a practical guide

You don't need a backyard. Balconies, windowsills, and vertical wall systems have turned city apartments into productive micro-farms for millions of people.

Pocket planters mounted on south-facing walls can support up to 40 plants in under four square feet. Strawberries, herbs, lettuce, and cherry tomatoes all thrive vertically with simple drip irrigation setups that cost under $30 to assemble.

Hydroponics at home

Modern hydroponic kits start around $80 and produce a continuous supply of leafy greens on a kitchen counter. Plants grow 30 to 50 percent faster without soil and use around 90 percent less water. The main upfront cost is the grow light if your space doesn't get direct sun.

Community garden waiting lists in most major cities now stretch two to three years. Cities including Singapore, Tokyo, and Amsterdam have begun integrating green growing space into building codes for any new construction above a certain height.

The mental health case for urban growing is increasingly well-documented. Regular contact with living plants correlates with lower cortisol levels and reduced reported anxiety — effects that appear even with small container gardens on a windowsill. See the research.

Music theory for people who gave up on it

The circle of fifths sounds intimidating until the moment it clicks. After that, you'll start seeing the same patterns in almost every song you've ever loved.

Nearly a third of all popular music in the last century sits on the same four-chord sequence. In C major: C, F, G, back to C. Play those four chords in any order, and you're already making something recognisable. The theory exists to explain why it works, not to complicate it.

Major vs. minor

The difference between a major and a minor chord is a single note moved down one half step. That one change shifts the emotional colour of the entire chord — from resolved and open to tense and inward. Understanding that makes transposing between keys intuitive rather than memorised.

Scales give you a palette. Chords are the brushstrokes. Once you've internalised the natural chords of a key, improvisation starts to feel like a conversation rather than a calculation. Your fingers start suggesting things your ears then confirm.

The most effective way to learn is not through exercises but through songs you already know. Pick five songs you can hum. Find their chords. You'll notice the patterns within the first hour. Recommended starting songs.

Science this week: proteins, constants, and sleep

Three findings worth knowing about — protein folding applications, a new Hubble constant measurement, and what sleep does to immune memory.

AlphaFold's structural predictions are now being used to design novel enzymes that break down plastics at room temperature. Early lab results show degradation rates twelve times higher than any previously known organism. Scale remains an open problem.

The Hubble tension, again

A new gravitational lensing measurement puts the Hubble constant at 73.4 km/s/Mpc — significantly above the 67.4 predicted by early-universe CMB data. The discrepancy is now at 5-sigma. At that confidence level, either the measurement method has a systematic error nobody has found, or the standard model needs revision.

Sleep and vaccine response

A Stanford study found that immune T-cells encoding pathogen memory consolidate during slow-wave sleep. Subjects sleeping fewer than six hours showed 40 percent weaker responses to novel antigens three months post-vaccination. The effect held after controlling for age, weight, and baseline immune health. Full paper.

The exposure triangle, explained without the jargon

Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. Understanding how these three interact gives you complete creative control over any camera in any situation.

Aperture controls depth of field. A wide aperture (f/1.8) blurs the background and isolates your subject. A narrow aperture (f/11) keeps everything from foreground to horizon in focus. For portraits, open up. For landscapes, close down.

Shutter speed and motion

Fast shutters (1/1000s and above) freeze motion. A sprinter mid-stride, a bird's wings, a splash of water — all sharp. Slow shutters (1/15s and below) let motion blur across the frame. Waterfalls turn silky. Headlights become light trails. Both are tools, not mistakes.

ISO and noise

ISO amplifies the sensor's signal. Higher ISO means more sensitivity in low light, but also more digital noise. Modern cameras handle ISO 3200 acceptably well. A slightly noisy photo is almost always better than a blurry one.

Composition rules — rule of thirds, leading lines, framing within a frame — are starting points, not laws. The best photographers know the rules well enough to break them deliberately. Composition exercises.
