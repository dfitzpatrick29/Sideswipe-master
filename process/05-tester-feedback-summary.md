Sideswipe — Usability Evaluation
Daniel Fitzpatrick, grayson mackle, dylan eytan
	Tester Demographics
Six testers participated in this evaluation. Testers ranged in comfort with webcam-based tools from moderate to high. All testers interacted with the live Sideswipe demo page in a real browser with a functioning webcam, as required.

Testing Scenario
Testers were given the following scenario before interacting with Sideswipe:

“You are working on a laptop in a shared study space where your hands are occupied — you are eating lunch and need to browse between tabs, scroll a page, and open a new tab, all without touching the keyboard or mouse. A small camera-based tool called Sideswipe is running on your computer. Try to figure out how to control your browser using only your hands in front of the webcam.”

** Observation Notes
No tester discovered the activation gesture (double clap) without prompting, and most relied on the gesture reference dropdown to understand the controls — those who read it succeeded significantly faster.
Tab-switching gestures (left and right swipes) were the most naturally discovered by testers.
** The usability test revealed three areas for revision:
Make the gesture reference panel expanded by default when the app is first opened; one tester specifically asked for “some kind of box where I could see controls.”
Improve scrolling accuracy — the scroll gesture was the most criticized element, with testers noting lag and unclear movement; a visual cue (e.g., an on-screen indicator) would help confirm the input was registered.
Add gesture confirmation feedback — testers could not tell when a gesture was registered versus missed; a brief on-screen label (e.g., “Swipe right detected”) would close the feedback loop and reduce false positives.
Overall, the evaluation was positive: 6/6 testers said the tool accomplished its goal and the 3.5/4 average rating reflects general enthusiasm, but onboarding and feedback mechanisms need strengthening before wider deployment.
